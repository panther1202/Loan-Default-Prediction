# ADR-0007: Approval Decision, Pricing & Counterfactual Suggestions

**Status:** Accepted. Parameters confirmed by Panth on 2026-09-03 after measuring each candidate against the full 255,347-row dataset; the original proposed defaults changed on three of four counts as a result — see Confirmed Parameters and Evidence below.
**Date:** 2026-09-03
**Deciders:** Panth

## Context

The intended user flow: an applicant fills out a full form (profile + desired loan), the system decides approve/deny, and if approved, states an interest rate and loan term; if denied, gives a reason and a "might help" suggestion.

Two problems, both confirmed empirically against the actual model (`model_params.json`), not assumed:

1. `InterestRate` and `LoanTerm` are **inputs** the frozen model was trained to score against, not outputs it produces. There is no "what rate should we offer" logic anywhere in the existing repo — this is new decision logic, not something to extract from the pickle files.
2. The obvious design — raise the offered rate for riskier applicants to price in the risk — makes this specific model's predicted default probability *worse*, not better. A sweep at a fixed applicant profile: `P(default)` rises from 0.333 at 4% APR to 0.603 at 20% APR, monotonically. The model reads rate as a marker of risk (from whatever process generated the training data's historical rates), not a dial that offsets it. Loan term has a comparatively negligible effect (~1 point of `P(default)` across the full 12–60 month range).

## Decision

**Rate is assigned by a simple, transparent, deterministic rate card (credit-score band × requested term) — never searched for via the model.** The model's only job is to gate approve/deny at that assigned rate, for the applicant's requested amount and term. No upward rate search, because the evidence above shows it never helps on this model — the lowest rate the card allows is also always the applicant's best chance of approval.

**Approval threshold:** `P(default) ≤ 0.37` — approve at the assigned rate if under it, deny if over. This is a risk-appetite choice, not a derived fact: 0.50 (sklearn's own default boundary) was the original proposal, and Panth chose 0.37 to be more conservative.

**On denial, suggestions are computed, not guessed by the LLM.** For an explicit allow-list of features, compute the minimum single-feature change (holding everything else fixed) that would flip `P(default)` below the threshold, and surface the 1–2 most feasible ones. The LLM's only job is phrasing them with appropriate hedging ("might help," "estimated, not guaranteed") — the number itself is exact linear-model math, same as the explanation layer.

**Allow-listed suggestion features:** requested `LoanAmount` (reduce), `HasCoSigner` (add one), `CreditScore` (directional target, phrased as an estimate). `LoanTerm` was in this list as “extend” and has been removed — measurement showed extending is worth 0.9% and only *shortening* helps, which is not advice worth giving (see Evidence 3). **Never** `Age`, `MaritalStatus`, `EmploymentType`, or `Education` — confirmed via the same sweep that `Age` alone swings `P(default)` from 0.558 (age 22) to 0.161 (age 70), by far the model's most powerful lever. That's exactly why it's the one lever this system will never mention, regardless of how "helpful" it would mathematically be to suggest.

## Confirmed parameters

**Approval threshold:** `P(default) ≤ 0.37` → approve, `> 0.37` → deny. Chosen by Panth over
sklearn's default 0.50 as a more conservative risk appetite. Note this is not "37% chance of
default" in a calibrated sense: the model was fit with `class_weight='balanced'` against an ~11%
base rate, so its probabilities are shifted upward relative to real-world frequency. 0.37 is a
decision boundary on this model's output, not a calibrated default rate.

**Credit-score bands:** quintiles of the observed `CreditScore` distribution, breakpoints
**409 / 519 / 629 / 740**, exported to `model_params.json.credit_score_bands` and regenerated by
`export_model_params.py` whenever the underlying data changes. Each band holds ~20% of applicants
(19.8 / 20.1 / 20.3 / 20.0 / 19.8%).

This replaces the originally proposed FICO-conventional bands (750+ / 700-749 / 650-699 / 600-649 /
<600). `CreditScore` in this dataset is uniform over 300-849, not FICO-shaped, so the conventional
bands put **54.8% of all applicants into the bottom band** paying the highest rate. The
realistic-looking bands were quietly the most punitive option available.

**Rate card** (one rate per band, no term dimension):

| CreditScore band | APR |
|---|---|
| 740+ | 7.0% |
| 629–739 | 8.0% |
| 519–628 | 9.0% |
| 409–518 | 10.0% |
| <409 | 11.0% |

**Term options offered:** 12, 24, 36, 48, 60 months, matching the dataset's observed `LoanTerm`
range exactly. No rate surcharge for longer terms (see Evidence 2).

**Suggestion allow-list, on denial:** requested `LoanAmount` (reduce), `HasCoSigner` (add one),
`CreditScore` (directional target, phrased as an estimate). `LoanTerm` is **removed** from the
originally proposed list (see Evidence 3). Still never `Age`, `MaritalStatus`, `EmploymentType`, or
`Education`.

## Evidence for the parameter changes

**Read these as measurements of this model on this data, not as findings about lending:** [[0009-training-data-source]] establishes that `data/Loan_default.csv` is synthetic, with uniform marginals on every numeric column. That is what makes `CreditScore` nearly unpredictive and `EstimatedEMI`'s sign run backwards below. The numbers are correct and are the right basis for the current build; they are not consumer-credit insight.

All figures below come from replaying the frozen model over the dataset (40,000-row sample for
the card figures, 20,000 for the suggestion levers) at threshold 0.37. The replay is verified
against `logistic_regression_loan_default_model.pkl` itself to a maximum absolute difference of
`1.1e-16`, so these are the deployed model's own numbers rather than an approximation of them.

### 1. The rate card was doing more of the deciding than the model

Approval rate by band at 36 months, originally proposed card vs. a flat 6.5% for everyone:

| | 750+ | 700–749 | 650–699 | 600–649 | <600 | ALL |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| proposed card (6.5–16.0%) | 72.2% | 65.2% | 53.6% | 45.1% | 30.6% | 44.6% |
| flat 6.5% for everyone | 72.2% | 69.7% | 65.8% | 64.9% | 59.2% | 63.6% |

The model's own credit signal separates the best band from the worst by 13 points. The proposed card
stretched that to 41 points — so roughly two-thirds of the risk discrimination came from the card's
rate spread rather than from the model.

That is circular in a way that matters for an explainability project. The card keys entirely on
`CreditScore`, which is a top-3 attribution in only **0.8%** of denials (coefficient −0.126). It
then assigns `InterestRate`, coefficient **+0.460** — the model's second-strongest feature. A wide
card converts a feature the model nearly ignores into the one that decides the outcome, and the
denial reason becomes a term the lender chose. For scale: dropping one applicant's rate from 10.5%
to 6.5% rescues **25.1%** of denials, the most powerful single lever in the whole system.

**Resolution:** compress the spread to 400bps (7.0–11.0%). Band separation becomes 28.1 points —
roughly half model, half card — at 56.0% overall approval. Shifting the whole card by 1% moves
overall approval about 3 points the other way, which is the dial to reach for if the approval rate
needs tuning later.

### 2. The term surcharge cost nothing and bought nothing

The proposed +0.5% APR on 48/60-month loans is worth **+6.5 bps** of `P(default)` (measured in band
700–749: 0.3186 → 0.3252 at 36 months). It is not meaningfully priced risk on this model. It also
taxes the longer terms that are already the worse direction (see 3), so it was dropped rather than
kept as decoration. One rate per band is also one fewer dimension for a Stage 2 citation to explain.

### 3. "Extend your loan term" was backwards on this model

Rescue rate over the denied population (36 months, 10.5% APR, n=9,577 denials):

| Lever | Denials flipped to approve |
|---|---|
| `LoanAmount` −50% | 23.1% |
| `HasCoSigner` → Yes | 12.0% |
| `LoanTerm` 36 → 12 (**shorten**) | 10.5% |
| `CreditScore` +100 | 7.5% |
| `LoanTerm` 36 → 60 (**extend**, as originally allow-listed) | 0.9% |

A shorter term means a larger monthly payment, and `EstimatedEMI` carries coefficient **−0.240** —
so on this model a bigger payment *lowers* predicted default, presumably because the training data's
larger payments belong to applicants who could carry them. Extending the term, the financially
sensible advice, is worth almost nothing (0.9%).

That leaves two honest options and no good third one: tell a denied applicant to raise their monthly
payment (correct on this model, bad advice for the person), or say nothing about term at all.
`LoanTerm` was therefore dropped from the suggestion allow-list. The applicant still chooses their
term freely; the system simply never suggests changing it as a remedy. This quirk is recorded here
rather than worked around silently, because it is a property of the frozen model that any future
retrain should be re-checked against (see [[0008-model-change-policy]]).

### 4. Side finding for Stage 1

`Age` is a top-3 attribution in **76.0%** of denials, so the protected-feature filter from
[[0005-ml-model-hosting]] fires on the large majority of cases rather than occasionally. What stays
citable after filtering is `MonthsEmployed` (top-3 in 55.2%) and `LoanAmount` (43.0%) — real
material, so the filter does not starve the explanation. `InterestRate` reaches top-3 in only 10.8%
of denials despite its large coefficient, because card rates cluster near the training mean of
13.49%. It is left un-filtered, but the circularity in 1 is a reason to keep the spread narrow, not
a reason to rely on it rarely surfacing.

## Consequences

- Stage 1 (`docs/IMPLEMENTATION_PLAN.md`) expands from "explain a given decision" to "assign a rate via the rate card → score the model → decide approve/deny → explain, and on denial, compute suggestions." All of this is still plain arithmetic in the Worker — no new hosting/architecture implications beyond what ADR-0005 already established.
- The rate card is itself a policy document — a natural fit for the Stage 2 RAG corpus, so the explanation can cite "why this rate" from the same retrieval mechanism that grounds the denial reasons.
- This reintroduces the counterfactual/"what-if" capability that was explicitly deferred earlier in scoping — worth naming plainly rather than pretending it's unrelated. It's scoped down from a free-form agent tool to a fixed, pre-computed suggestion on the denial path, with a hard-coded feature allow-list, which is a meaningfully smaller and safer surface than the original open-ended version.
- `docs/TEST_STRATEGY.md` needs a new check: suggestions must only ever reference allow-listed features, with the same 100%-hard-gate treatment as the protected-feature exclusion check on explanations.
